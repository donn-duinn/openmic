# Who actually owns Melbourne's open mic venues

Compiled 2026-07-29. Public record only. Every ownership claim below traces to
a named primary source. Where the record does not answer the question, this
document says **unclear** rather than guessing.

**Nothing here is a claim about anyone's finances, tax affairs or private
dealings.** It is a map of who holds the licence and who signs the contract.

---

## Headline finding

**Effectively none of the ~45 target venues are owned by a big pub group.**

Cross-checking every target address against the full Victorian liquor licence
register, each target venue's licensee holds either one licence or two. Not one
of them is licensed to Australian Venue Co, ALH/Endeavour, Open Door Pub Co or
any other multi-venue operator.

That is the opposite of what you might assume, and it is good news: **the whole
target list is owner-operator rooms.** The decision-maker is the publican, the
licensee or the promoter — not a head office. There is no big group worth one
consolidated pitch, because no group is in this market. Open mic nights live in
small independent bars, and small independent bars decide on the spot.

The three exceptions where one conversation covers two rooms are listed in
[One conversation, two rooms](#one-conversation-two-rooms).

## Method and sources

Three public registers, cross-referenced:

1. **Liquor Control Victoria — Current Victorian Licences By Location, 30 April
   2026.** The authoritative register of who is licensed to sell alcohol at a
   given address. 23,763 current licences.
   - Dataset page: <https://discover.data.vic.gov.au/dataset/victorian-liquor-licences-by-location>
   - File: <https://www.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-05/Current_Victorian_Licences_By_Location-30-April-2026.xlsx>
   - Gives: licence number, **licensee**, trading name, category, **maximum
     capacity**, address, council.
2. **ABN Lookup** (Australian Business Register) — <https://abr.business.gov.au/>
   Gives: entity name behind a trading name, entity type (private company /
   sole trader / trust / incorporated association), main business location.
3. **Company and venue websites, and news reporting**, cited inline.

Note that (1) and (2) are **different registers**. The liquor licensee and the
business-name holder are often different entities for the same venue — see Bar
Oussou and Ms Carlisle's below. Where they differ, both are listed.

### Two caveats you must not ignore

- **A single licence does not prove independence.** Several genuine groups run
  each venue through a separate company, so the group name never appears in the
  register. Proof: Sand Hill Road's Waterside Hotel is licensed to *Midtown
  Hustle Pty Ltd*, and searching "Sand Hill", "Colonial Leisure", "PHMG" and
  "Kirk" in the register returns nothing at all. What the register *does* prove
  is that none of the target venues share a licensee with a known group, and
  that their licensee entities are single-venue. Combined with the ABN entity
  names (mostly named after the venue or its street address), independence is
  the strong reading — but treat it as "no evidence of group ownership" rather
  than a guarantee.
- **The register is a 30 April 2026 snapshot.** Venues open, close and change
  hands. Verify before you pitch.

**The pattern worth acting on:** there are dozens of member-run clubs across the
inner north and west with big rooms, cheap hire and no live music programming.
They are not on your target list because they do not run open mics *yet*. A club
committee looking for a reason to open on a Tuesday is a different sale from a
publican — you are pitching a program, not a tool.

**Which body would your customer belong to?** A small independent bar running an
open mic is far more likely to be in **Music Victoria** (or the free ALMBC micro
tier) than the AHA. Pitch through Music Victoria's venue roundtable, not the AHA.

## What is still unclear

Stated plainly, because guessing here would be worse than useless:

- Who the **individual shareholders and directors** are behind the private
  companies. That needs a paid ASIC company extract; ABN Lookup does not show it.
- Ownership of **Colonial Leisure Group, Public House Management Group and
  Kirk's** venues — these use per-venue companies and do not appear in the
  register under a group name.
- Whether **The Tote** is community or crowd-owned; who owns **Brunswick
  Ballroom**; who owns **The Curtin**.
- Trading status of the five venues with no licence match.
- Whether the **comedy promoters** (Hard Knock Knocks' listed rooms) hold any
  formal relationship with the venues, or simply hire the room. For your
  purposes the promoter is probably the buyer either way.
