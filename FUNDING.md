## 1. Music Works — Project Grants ($10,000–$40,000) — BEST FIT, CLOSES IN DAYS

- **Funder:** Victorian Government (Creative Victoria), delivered by Music Victoria.
- **What it funds:** Victorian contemporary music artists, music workers, music
  businesses and organisations undertaking creative and professional development
  projects. The Project stream is explicitly for *"strategic projects which deliver
  broader artistic, business and sector outcomes such as industry development,
  cross-industry collaboration, and **innovative products, services and
  technologies**."* That last clause is this project, almost word for word.
- **Amount:** $10,000–$40,000 (Project stream).
- **Eligibility:** "Victorian artists, music workers, music businesses and
  organisations." An individual/sole trader music worker qualifies. **No
  incorporation or auspice required.**
- **Dates:** Opened 10am Wed 8 July 2026. **Closes 2pm Wed 5 August 2026 (AEST).**
  Outcomes from October 2026. Eligible project dates 1 Nov 2026 – 30 Oct 2027.
- **Apply:** https://www.musicvictoria.com.au/initiatives/music-works/ →
  Grants Portal https://musicvictoria.awardsplatform.com/
- **Guidelines / program page:**
  https://creative.vic.gov.au/funding-opportunities/find-a-funding-opportunity/music-works
- **Enquiries:** musicworks@musicvictoria.com.au. They offer 15-minute booked
  appointments with a Music Works team member — book one immediately if any slots
  remain before close.
- **Why it ranks first:** the funder is the peak body for exactly this sector, the
  stream language names the thing being built, the project is already live and
  demonstrable, and the fair-pay/rights angle aligns with Music Victoria's own
  advocacy positions. This is the single highest-probability application available.
- **Realism:** the deadline is days away. A Project application needs a budget,
  outcomes, and a real delivery plan. If that can't be done properly in the time,
  do not submit a thin one — drop to the Activation stream (below), which is
  designed for less detailed applications, and put a full Project application in
  at the next round.

## 3. Creative Australia — Arts Projects for Individuals and Groups ($10,000–$50,000)

## 7. 10,000 Gigs: The Victorian Gig Fund — the strategic one

- **Funder:** Victorian Government, delivered by Music Victoria. Third round.
- **What it funds:** **Victorian venues** to stage more paid live music by local
  artists. Not artists directly.
- **Dates:** listed by Creative Victoria under "Coming up" — "Applications open in
  2026." Guidelines and key dates "to be announced shortly" as at late July 2026.
- **Register for updates:** https://click.musicvictoria.com.au/n/forms/333587/REvnrh
- **Announcement:** https://www.musicvictoria.com.au/funding-announced-for-victorias-music-industry/
- **Why it matters even though he can't apply:** this is the single strongest venue
  pitch available. A venue applying to fund paid local gigs needs a sign-up system,
  a running order, evidence of who played and proof that artists got paid. That is
  exactly what has been built, and it is free. Approach target venues with "I will
  help you apply to 10,000 Gigs, and my system is the acquittal paperwork." That
  converts the tool from a favour into the thing that unlocks their funding.
  It also builds a case study and a relationship with Music Victoria for the next
  Music Works round.

## 8. Australian Cultural Fund — not a grant, but the sustainability answer

- **Operator:** Creative Australia. ATO-endorsed deductible gift recipient (DGR).
- **What it is:** a fundraising platform for Australian artists. The artist uploads
  a project and raises tax-deductible donations. Not all-or-nothing — if the goal
  isn't met, donations still flow.
- **Eligibility:** individual Australian artists. **No incorporation, no charity
  status, no auspice needed.** This is the only realistic way a sole trader offers
  donors a tax deduction.
- **Open:** continuously.
- **URL:** https://australianculturalfund.org.au/
- **Why it ranks here:** it directly solves the stated problem — funding the thing
  without charging artists or venues. Venues, punters and industry people who like
  the project can fund it and get a deduction, with no company set up and no
  membership fee anywhere in the product.

## ⚠ The incorporation question — read this before deciding

This determines which half of the money is reachable.

**Open to him as a sole trader, right now, no structural change:**

- Music Works (both streams) — "artists, music workers, businesses"
- Creative Australia Arts Projects for Individuals and Groups — individuals only
- Merri-bek Arts Projects Grant, Individual Artists category — auspice explicitly
  not required, ABN required
- City of Melbourne Annual Arts Grants — individual artists eligible (round closed)
- City of Melbourne Connected Neighbourhood Small Grants — individuals eligible
- Australian Cultural Fund — individual artists

**Likely to require incorporation or an auspicing body:**

- Yarra Community Grants medium and large streams (unverified — confirm)
- Darebin Community Grants (unverified — confirm)
- Merri-bek Arts Projects Grant, *Organisations* category — must be a not-for-profit
  incorporated company or association, or a First Peoples business. **For-profit
  arts organisations are ineligible unless First Peoples-owned.** Note what that
  means: incorporating as a normal *for-profit* company would make him *less*
  eligible here than he is now as an individual artist.
- City of Melbourne Social Investment Partnership Grants
- Greater Melbourne Foundation and philanthropic trusts generally — usually charity
  or DGR status

**The honest read.** The two largest and best-fitting opportunities — Music Works
Project Grants ($40k) and Creative Australia Arts Projects ($50k) — are both open
to him exactly as he is. There is no funding case for incorporating in the next
six months. If a structure is eventually needed, the one that unlocks the most
locked doors is a **not-for-profit incorporated association** (Consumer Affairs
Victoria), not a Pty Ltd — a Pty Ltd unlocks almost nothing on this list and
actively closes the Merri-bek organisation category. That said, incorporation is
also a governance and liability question, not only a funding one: MASTER.md open
question 3 flags that the directory publishes opinions about businesses and the
system holds personal data. Decide that on legal advice, not on grants.

**Middle path if a big council grant looks winnable:** an auspice. A local
incorporated arts organisation or neighbourhood house holds the funds and takes
the reporting burden, usually for a 5–10% admin fee, and he delivers the project.
Faster and cheaper than incorporating, and reversible.

## Being realistic about the odds

- **Music Works** is the best chance on this list and it closes in days. Everything
  else should wait until that is either submitted or consciously skipped.
- **Council grants are oversubscribed by roughly 2:1** on Darebin's own published
  numbers, and Yarra says the same in plainer words. Councils also strongly prefer
  projects delivered *inside their own boundaries* for *their own residents* —
  a state-wide or Melbourne-wide software tool reads as out of scope. If applying
  to a council, apply for **a specific series of nights at named venues in that
  municipality**, with the platform as the method, not the deliverable. That single
  reframe is the difference between eligible and not.
- **Creative Australia is national and all art forms.** Expect low odds. Apply
  anyway — the registration deadline (31 Aug) costs nothing to meet, and peer
  feedback from a failed round is genuinely useful.
- **The strongest long-term position is not a grant at all.** It is being the
  system that venues use to win 10,000 Gigs money and acquit it. That builds the
  track record, the venue relationships and the Music Victoria standing that make
  every subsequent application easier. Grants reward a demonstrated thing; this is
  already live at openmic.techduinn.dpdns.org, which is a real advantage
  over the many applicants pitching an idea.
- **One caution on framing.** MASTER.md is right that disclosed self-interest
  survives and discovered self-interest does not. Grant applications are public
  documents assessed by industry peers who know this scene. The "I'm a musician, I
  want to run nights, and I may book people from this scene" line belongs in the
  application too, not only on the sign-up page. Assessors reward candour and
  punish discovering it themselves.
